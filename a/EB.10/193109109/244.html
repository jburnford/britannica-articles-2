GRUBBING

article · 852 chars · lineage ↗ · page image at NLS ↗

GRUBBING, in Agriculture, the digging or pulling up of the stubs and roots of trees. When the roots are large, this is a laborious task: but Mr Mortimer hath shewn how it may be accomplished in such a manner as to save great expence by a very simple and easy method. He proposes a strong iron hook to be made about two feet four inches long, with a large iron ring fastened to the upper part of it. This hook must be fastened in a hole in the side of the root; and a lever being put into the ring, three men, by means of this lever, may wring out the root, and twist the sap-roots asunder. Stubs of trees may also be taken up with the same hook, in which work it will save a great deal of labour, though not so much as in the other; because the stubs must be first cleft with wedges, before the hook can enter their sides, to wrench them out by pieces.
