HURDLES

article · 763 chars · lineage ↗ · page image at NLS ↗

HURDLES, in Fortification, are made of twigs of
willows or osiers interwoven close together, fastened
by long stakes. They are made in the figure of a
long square, the length being five or six feet, and the
breadth three and a half. The closer they are wattled
together, the better. They serve to render the bat-
teries firm, or to consolidate the passage over muddy
ditches; or to cover traverses and lodgements for the
defence of the workmen against fire-works or stones
thrown against them.

The Romans had a kind of military execution for
mutineers, called putting to death under the hurdle.
The manner of it was this: The criminal was laid at
his length in a shallow water, under an hurdle, upon
which was heaped stones, and so pressed down till he
was drowned.
