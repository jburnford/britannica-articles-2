FOLLY

article · 373 chars · lineage ↗ · page image at NLS ↗

FOLLY, according to Mr Locke, consists in the drawing of false conclusions from just principles; by which it is distinguished from madness, which draws just conclusions from false principles.

But this seems too confined a definition; folly, in its most general acceptance, denoting a weakness of intellect or apprehension, or some partial absurdity in sentiment or conduct.
