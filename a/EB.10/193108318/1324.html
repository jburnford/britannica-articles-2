BAWD

BAWD, a person who keeps a place of prostitution, or makes a trade of debauching women, and procuring or conducting of criminal intrigues. Some think the word is derived from the old French Baud, bold or impudent; though Verslegan has a conjecture which would carry it higher, viz. from bothe anciently written bade. In which sense bawd originally imported no more than bath-holder, as if bagnios had anciently been the chief scenes of such prostitution.

The Romans had their male as well as female bawds; the former denominated leones and provozi, among us panders; the latter, lene. Donatus, speaking of the habits of the ancient characters in comedy, says, Leno palii varii coloris utitur. But the ancient lenones, it is to be observed, furnished boys as well as girls for venereal service. Another sort of these merchants or dealers in human flesh, were called mangones, by the Greeks αδρακατλη, who sold eunuchs, slaves, &c. By a law of Constantine, bawds were to be punished by pouring melted lead down their throats. See the next article.
