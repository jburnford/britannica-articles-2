FURIES, in Pagan antiquity, certain goddesses whose office it was to punish the guilty after death. They were three in number: Alesto, Megæra, and Tisiphone; who were described with snakes instead of hair, and eyes like lightning, carrying iron chains and whips in one hand, and in the other flaming torches; the latter to discover, and the former to punish, the guilty: and they were supposed to be constantly hovering over such persons as had been guilty of any enormous crime.
