LESBOS

LESBOS, a large island in the Ægean sea, on the coast of Ætolia, about 168 miles in circumference. It has been several times called Pelasgia, from the Pelasgi, by whom it was first peopled; Macaria, from Macareus who settled in it; and Lesbos, from the son-in-law and

successor of Macareus, who bore the same name. The chief towns of Lemnos were Methymna and Mitylene. It was originally governed by kings, but they were afterwards subjected to the neighbouring powers. The wine which it produced was greatly esteemed by the ancients, and still is in the same repute with the moderns. The Lesbians were so debauched and dissipated, that the epithet of Lesbian was often used to signify debauchery and extravagance. Lesbos has given birth to many illustrious persons, such as Arion, Terpander, Sappho, &c. See MITYLENE.
