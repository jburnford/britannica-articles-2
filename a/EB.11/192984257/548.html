INFATUATE

INFATUATE, to prepossess any one in favour of some person or thing that does not deserve it, so far as that he cannot easily be disabused.—The word infatuate comes from the Latin fatuus, "fool;" of fari, "to speak out," which is borrowed from the Greek φᾶν, whence φᾶν, which signifies the same with rates in Latin, or prophet in English; and the reason is, because their prophecies or priests used to be seized with a kind of madness or folly, when they began to make their predictions, or deliver oracles.
