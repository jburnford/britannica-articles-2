SELEUCIA

article · 820 chars · lineage ↗ · page image at NLS ↗

SELEUCIA, in Ancient Geography, surnamed Babylonia, because situated on its confines, at the confluence of the Euphrates and Tigris. Ptolemy places it in Mesopotamia. It is called also Seleucia ad Tigrim, (Polybius, Strabo, Isidorus Characenus); washed on the south by the Euphrates, on the east by the Tigris, (Theophylactus); generally agreed to have been built or enlarged by Seleucus Nicator, master of the east after Alexander; by means of which Babylon came to be deserted. It is said to have been originally called Coche, (Amian, Eutropius); though others, as Arrian, distinguish it, as a village, from Seleucia; and, according to Zosimus, the ancient name of Seleucia was Zochasita. Now called Bagdad. E. Long. 44. 21. N. Lat. 33. 10. There were many other cities of the same name, all built by Seleucus Nicator.
