PATÆCI, in Mythology, images of gods which the Phœnicians carried on the prows of their gallees. Herodotus, lib. iv. calls them παταικας. The word is Phœnician, and derived from pethica, i. e. titulus. See Bocchart's Chanaan, lib. ii. cap. 3. But Scaliger does not agree. Morin derives it from παικας, monkey, this animal having been an object of worship among the Egyptians, and hence might have been honoured by their
