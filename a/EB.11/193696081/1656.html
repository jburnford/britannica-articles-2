WIND-Taught

sub_entry · 464 chars · lineage ↗ · page image at NLS ↗

WIND-Taught, in sea language, denotes the same as stiff in the wind. Too much rigging, high masts, or any thing catching or holding wind aloft, is said to hold a ship wind-taught; by which they mean, that she stoops too much in her sailing in a stiff gale of wind. Again, when a ship rides in a main stress of wind and weather, they strike down her top masts, and bring her yards down, which else would hold too much wind, or be too much distended and wind-taught.
