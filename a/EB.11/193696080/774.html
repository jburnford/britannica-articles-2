BALLISTEUM

article · 988 chars · lineage ↗ · page image at NLS ↗

BALLISTEUM, or BALLISTREA, in antiquity, a military song or dance used on occasions of victory. Vopiscus has preserved the balisteum sung in honour of Aurelian, who, in the Sarmatian war, was said to have

Ballisteum || Balloon. have killed 48 of the enemy in one day with his own hand. Mille, mille, mille, mille, mille, mille decollavimus: Unus homo mille, mille, mille, mille decollavit; mille, mille, mille vivat, qui mille, mille occidit. Tantum vini habet nemo, quantum fudit sanguinis. The same writer subjoins another popular song of the same kind: Mille Fracos, mille Sarmatas, semel occidimus; mille, mille, mille, mille, mille, Persas, quarimus. It took the denomination ballisteum from the Greek βαλλω, jacio, or jacto, to cast or toss, on account of the motions used in this dance, which was attended with great elevations and swingings of the hands. The ballistea were a kind of popular ballads, composed by poets of the lower class, without much regard to the laws of metre.
